About Me

My path to education policy runs through Bangladesh and the development sector. I started as a research associate studying microfinance regulation, then spent four years at the Palli Karma-Sahayak Foundation running skills-for-employment programs for people in underserved communities. In 2019 I joined Hajee Mohammad Danesh Science and Technology University as an Assistant Professor and, in 2021, became Chair of the Department of Development Studies. Leading a department with limited resources showed me how often leaders have to make consequential decisions without good evidence, and that is what brought me to the University of Arkansas to study education policy. Outside my research, I care about opening doors for students: I have mentored more than 15 into fully funded graduate programs abroad, and I founded the Bangladesh Open Learning Society, which gave 10,000+ members free access to online courses during COVID-19.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Education Policy at the University of Arkansas, where I am a Doctoral Academy Fellow, and a School Finance Fellow with EdFund. My research spans education finance, the economics of education, teacher labor markets, and higher education, examining the institutional and policy factors that shape how educational resources are allocated.

I use causal inference, applied econometrics, and machine learning to study how education policy and governance shape the decisions and outcomes of students, educators, and school systems. My work is motivated by a simple question: How can rigorous empirical evidence help policymakers and education leaders make better decisions? My current research examines the fiscal consequences and efficiency implications of small-district consolidation in Arkansas.
