Teaching philosophy

My teaching and mentoring aim to build one habit: using evidence critically in every consequential decision.

I teach students to apply evidence, not just read it. Where implementation is not possible, I help them ask of every proposal: will it hold here, at this scale, with this budget? They learn to weigh context, costs, incentives, and outcomes, judge whether a causal claim will transfer, and explain their conclusions to the school board member or legislator who must act on them.

How I Teach

A policy that works in Tennessee can fail in California: STAR’s class-size gains shrank when California scaled the policy statewide. I teach students to test whether evidence transfers.

Learning through practice

Students meet concepts in real settings: price journals in local markets, life-history interviews with low-income residents, and service-learning projects such as Backpacks of Hope. In policy courses, they will build a state funding formula from real district data and replicate a published finding in a new state.

Students as co-creators

Students frame their own questions. Groups teach a topic while others question them, and I grade both. Because many students are first-generation, I make expectations explicit, share model work, and break projects into graded steps.

Deliberate design

Syllabi follow Bloom’s taxonomy toward a policy memo, funding formula, or proposal. Classes are partly flipped, and lessons follow Gagné’s events of instruction: open with a real problem, close with a new application.

Student Feedback

From surveys I ran myself. Forty-one Development Economics students rated my instruction 9.1 out of 10, and Social Policy students rated it 90%.

Courses I Am Prepared to Teach

I can teach graduate courses in higher education policy and finance, governance and leadership, and research methods. I would also design an economics of higher education course in which M.Ed. students analyze a real funding or transfer decision and brief a college leader.

Introduction to Education Policy

Actors, institutions, and debates in K–12 and higher education policy, from finance and accountability to school choice.

Economics of Education

Human capital, cost-benefit analysis, and market design applied to returns to schooling and student achievement.

Education Finance

How revenue systems and funding formulas shape adequacy, equity, and resource allocation.

Quantitative Methods for Policy Analysis

Regression, causal inference, and working with administrative and survey data.

Program Evaluation

Applied Econometrics

Economics of Higher Education

Higher Education Governance and Leadership
